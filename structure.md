# 1. Project Overview

Sylva Auris 2.0 is a complete rebuild of the existing brand website.

The website should establish Sylva Auris as a premium, design-led terrarium and botanical art brand. It should communicate the brand's identity through an immersive visual experience while making its products, craftsmanship, materials and services easy to understand.

The website should not resemble a conventional ecommerce store, plant nursery or generic portfolio.

## Core Website Concept

The visitor enters through a forest, gradually discovers that the landscape exists within a terrarium, explores the products and craftsmanship, and eventually enquires about owning a composition.

The experience should combine:

- Immersive visual storytelling.
- Premium product presentation.
- Editorial layouts.
- Detailed craftsmanship and material information.
- Clear product discovery.
- A straightforward enquiry process.

## Primary Website Objectives

1. Establish a distinctive digital identity for Sylva Auris.
2. Communicate the brand's philosophy and design approach.
3. Showcase terrariums and kokedama.
4. Explain the materials and craftsmanship involved.
5. Present completed work through photography.
6. Generate individual, custom and bulk enquiries.
7. Educate customers about product care.

# 3. Homepage Structure

The homepage is the most important page of the website.

It should introduce the brand through a continuous visual narrative rather than presenting a sequence of conventional website sections.

## Section 01 — Hero

**Purpose:** Establish the first impression and introduce the brand.

### Layout
- Full-screen forest imagery.
- Minimal navigation.
- Sylva Auris wordmark.
- Tagline: "Forest. Reimagined."
- Subtle scroll indicator.

### Visual Direction
- Dark, cinematic forest environment.
- Natural lighting and atmospheric depth.
- Restrained typography.
- No conventional product cards.
- No unnecessary buttons or promotional elements.

### Interaction
- Subtle initial reveal.
- Smooth transition into the next section.
- Navigation remains accessible.

## Section 02 — Forest-to-Terrarium Transition

**Purpose:** Introduce the central concept behind Sylva Auris.

### Layout
- Forest imagery continues from the hero.
- Gradual visual transition.
- Glass edges begin to appear.
- Forest landscape progressively reveals itself as a terrarium.

### Interaction
- Scroll-linked image movement.
- Gradual scale and positioning changes.
- Controlled transitions between visual layers.

### Content
A short statement introducing the idea of bringing a miniature forest into a carefully composed environment.

This section should create the defining visual moment of the website.

## Section 03 — Brand Introduction

**Purpose:** Explain what Sylva Auris creates.

### Layout
- Large terrarium photograph.
- Short editorial text.
- Asymmetric image and text composition.

### Content
- Introduction to terrariums and kokedama.
- The relationship between nature and design.
- The importance of composition and detail.
- Brief introduction to the brand's approach.

Avoid excessive poetic language. The writing should be direct, confident and descriptive.

## Section 04 — Collection Preview

**Purpose:** Introduce the actual products.

### Layout
- Selected product photography.
- Large individual product presentations.
- Product names and essential details.
- Link to the complete collection.

### Content
Feature a limited selection of terrariums and kokedama.

Each preview should communicate:
- Product name.
- Product format.
- Main visual characteristics.
- Price, where confirmed.
- Link to product details or enquiry.

Avoid overcrowded ecommerce-style grids.

## Section 05 — The Art of Composition

**Purpose:** Explain what goes into creating a terrarium.

### Layout
Use detailed photography and carefully arranged content to introduce individual components.

### Content Categories

1. Plants
2. Moss
3. Roots and hardscape
4. Stone and gravel
5. Substrate
6. Glass vessels

Explain how these elements work together to create a balanced composition.

## Section 06 — Atelier / Customisation

**Purpose:** Introduce the customisation process.

### Layout
- Photography of the creation process.
- Close-up details of materials.
- Text explaining custom compositions.
- Link to the Atelier page.

### Content
- Custom terrarium commissions.
- Vessel selection.
- Plant and hardscape choices.
- Size and composition considerations.
- Enquiry process.

## Section 07 — Philosophy Preview

**Purpose:** Establish the thinking behind the brand.

### Layout
- Minimal product imagery.
- Short editorial statement.
- Link to the Philosophy page.

### Content
Introduce the brand's approach to nature, design, composition and detail.

## Section 08 — Closing Section

**Purpose:** End the homepage with a memorable visual and a clear next step.

### Layout
- One prominent terrarium photograph.
- Dark background.
- Minimal closing statement.
- Primary enquiry CTA.

### CTA
Explore the Collection / Make an Enquiry.

## Section 09 — Footer

Include:
- Sylva Auris wordmark.
- Tagline.
- Main navigation links.
- Care page.
- Instagram.
- Contact email.
- Location information, if confirmed.
- Copyright information.

# 12. Responsive Design Requirements

The website must be designed for desktop and mobile from the beginning.

## Desktop

- Immersive full-screen hero.
- Large cinematic imagery.
- Asymmetric editorial layouts.
- Scroll-linked visual transitions.
- Spacious product presentations.
- Minimal navigation.

## Tablet

- Adapted image and text compositions.
- Reduced animation complexity where necessary.
- Responsive navigation.
- Balanced spacing.

## Mobile

- Dedicated mobile layouts rather than simply shrinking desktop sections.
- Optimised image delivery.
- Compact navigation.
- Readable typography.
- Touch-friendly controls.
- Simplified scroll transitions.
- No horizontal overflow.
- Reduced-motion support.

---

# 13. Design and Interaction Principles

1. The homepage should be immersive, not cluttered.
2. Product pages should prioritise product information.
3. Atelier should communicate craftsmanship through detail.
4. Philosophy should communicate brand identity without excessive text.
5. Gallery should prioritise actual photography.
6. Care should prioritise usability.
7. Contact should make enquiries straightforward.
8. Animations should support the experience, not distract from it.
9. Avoid excessive gradients, glow effects, shimmer and cursor-following effects.
10. Do not use decorative divider lines between every section.
11. Maintain consistent typography, spacing and interaction behaviour.
12. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

- Final page list.

- Whether individual product detail pages are needed.
- Final hero photography.
- Exact forest-to-terrarium transition.
- Final homepage copy.
- Gallery categories.
- Enquiry form fields.
- Email delivery configuration.
- Current physical display and visiting information.
- Whether online purchasing will be introduced in the future.

Do not invent product details, prices, availability, delivery promises or business claims to fill these gaps.

---

# 16. Final Structural Recommendation

The website should be built around three major experiences:

**1. Discover — Homepage and Philosophy**

Establish the brand identity and introduce the concept behind Sylva Auris.

**2. Explore — Collection, Atelier and Gallery**

Show what the brand creates, how the compositions are made and the quality of the finished work.

**3. Connect — Contact and Enquiry**

Give visitors a straightforward way to enquire about ready-made products, custom compositions and gifting.

The homepage should carry the strongest visual storytelling. The Atelier should demonstrate the craft. The Collection should make products easy to explore. The remaining pages should provide supporting information and confidence.

The result should feel like a premium botanical design brand's digital experience, rather than a conventional online plant store.

# Sylva Auris --- Website Tone of Voice Guide

**Version:** 1.0\
**Purpose:** A practical editorial standard for the Sylva Auris website
and future brand communication.\
**Brand line:** *Forest. Reimagined.*

## 1. Purpose

Sylva Auris should sound like a considered botanical design studio:
knowledgeable about the natural elements it works with, attentive to
composition, and confident enough not to oversell.

The website must not read like generic luxury marketing or AI-generated
brand copy. It should sound as though someone who understands the work
has written it: someone who can explain why a plant, piece of wood,
layer of substrate or vessel belongs in a composition.

The website has three jobs:

1.  Help visitors feel the appeal of having a small, carefully composed
    natural landscape in their space.
2.  Help them understand what a terrarium is made of and how its
    elements work together.
3.  Explain what Sylva Auris offers, how to explore available creations,
    and how to enquire or visit in person.

**Core principle: Atmosphere should invite attention. Specific
information should reward it.**

### Editorial balance

Use these as broad proportions, not a rigid formula:

-   **Confidence --- 35%:** direct, composed statements.
-   **Atmosphere --- 30%:** sensory detail and a sense of place.
-   **Information --- 35%:** concrete descriptions and useful
    explanations.

A hero section may lean more atmospheric. A product description may need
more information. The overall website should retain all three.

## 3. What to learn from the supplied references

The supplied material contains extracted copy and observations from
Rolls-Royce Black Badge, The Tamara Coorg and Terraform. These are
references for writing techniques, not templates to imitate.

### Rolls-Royce Black Badge: confidence through restraint

The supplied copy uses short, declarative phrases such as "Dynamic.
Powerful. Potent." and "Create a Rolls-Royce wholly unique to you."

**Learn:** Lead with a clear thought. Use short sentences when a
statement deserves emphasis. Avoid repeatedly defending the brand's
value. Make customisation sound considered.

**Do not copy:** High-performance vocabulary, status-led exclusivity, or
the assumption that every line must sound dramatic. Sylva Auris should
be confident about its craft, not imitate a motor-car marque.

### The Tamara Coorg: atmosphere through specific context

The supplied copy connects the resort to the Western Ghats, coffee
plantations, architecture, materials and experiences. Its strongest
technique is connecting an offering to the environment around it.

**Learn:** Build atmosphere from real details. Describe natural forms
that inform a composition. Connect the object to the space in which it
will be observed. Use sensory language when it helps the reader picture
something.

**Do not copy:** Long resort-style descriptions, repeated words such as
*serene*, *tranquil*, *sanctuary* and *unforgettable*, or grand claims
about escape and wellness. For Sylva Auris, atmosphere can come from
moss, roots, foliage, texture and the way a landscape sits within glass.

### Terraform: clarity through explanation

The supplied copy explains its categories and services, including
terrariums, aquariums, paludariums, plant selection and hardscape
design.

**Learn:** Explain what is actually being made. Name the elements that
contribute to the finished composition. Make services and next steps
easy to understand. Prefer tangible descriptions over broad claims about
expertise.

**Do not copy:** Generic phrases such as "captivating experiences" and
"miniature masterpiece", broad claims of unmatched expertise, or
unsupported claims that every terrarium is a self-sustaining ecosystem.

### The Sylva Auris direction

Combine the restraint of Rolls-Royce, the use of real atmosphere found
in The Tamara's copy, and Terraform's willingness to explain the
offering. The result should be more specific than generic luxury copy
and more atmospheric than a product catalogue.

## 4. The central writing rule: write from the object outward

Start with what is genuinely present in the creation. Explain what it
contributes. Only then describe the feeling or impression it may create.

**Preferred order:**

1.  **The thing:** Name the actual element.
2.  **Its role:** Explain what it contributes to the composition.
3.  **The impression:** Describe what someone may notice or appreciate.

**Example**

> Moss forms the softer surfaces of the landscape, while roots and stone
> introduce structure and variation. Together, they give the composition
> the character of a forest floor in a much smaller setting.

**Avoid**

> Discover a breathtaking miniature paradise where nature and artistry
> unite in perfect harmony.

The first passage gives the reader something to picture and understand.
The second relies on familiar promotional language without explaining
the work.

Before keeping a sentence, ask: - Could this describe hundreds of
unrelated premium brands? - Does it tell the visitor something specific
about Sylva Auris or its creations? - Is the detail accurate? - Would
someone who makes or cares for the terrarium naturally say this?

If it could belong to any luxury brand, rewrite it around a real detail.

## 5. Word choice

### Words to favour

Use these when they describe something real. They are not mandatory
keywords.

**Composition and design:** composition, arrangement, form, proportion,
balance, structure, detail, vessel, shape, scale, placement, contrast,
texture, layered, considered, carefully arranged.

**Natural elements:** moss, fern, foliage, root, wood, stone, substrate,
plant, growth, forest floor, tropical landscape, ground cover, natural
form.

**Observation and experience:** observe, notice, explore, discover,
appreciate, look closer, bring into a space, sit within, take shape,
grow.

**Making and customisation:** create, compose, select, arrange, shape,
adapt, choose, customise, made to suit, developed around.

Use precise plant names when they are known and relevant. Do not insert
botanical terminology merely to sound expert.

### Words to use sparingly

These are not banned, but become weak when repeated or used without
evidence:

*luxury, premium, elegant, exquisite, beautiful, unique, timeless,
serene, tranquil, curated, bespoke, handcrafted, immersive, organic,
sustainable, masterpiece, sanctuary, experience.*

For example, instead of saying "a luxurious and elegant terrarium",
describe the qualities that make the piece feel considered: its
proportions, vessel, placement of hardscape and relationship between the
plants.

Use *bespoke* only where a genuinely custom process is offered. Use
*handcrafted* only where it accurately describes the work.

### AI-sounding phrases to avoid as defaults

-   "Where nature meets luxury."
-   "A harmonious blend of..."
-   "More than just a..."
-   "An exquisite celebration of..."
-   "Step into a world of..."
-   "Elevate your space with..."
-   "Transform your surroundings..."
-   "A perfect fusion of art and nature."
-   "Every detail tells a story."
-   "Crafted with passion and precision."
-   "Bringing the beauty of nature indoors."
-   "A sanctuary of serenity."
-   "Unparalleled beauty."
-   "Discover the magic of..."

These phrases are not automatically wrong in every context. They are
poor defaults because they are heavily used, abstract and
interchangeable between brands.

## 6. Sentence construction and rhythm

-   Prefer active voice.
-   Use familiar words before specialist terms.
-   Keep most sentences to one main idea.
-   Vary sentence length naturally.
-   Use an occasional short sentence for emphasis.
-   Do not make every sentence sound like a headline.
-   Avoid stacking three or four adjectives before a noun.
-   Do not force every section into the same paragraph length.

**Preferred**

> The vessel gives the composition its shape. Within it, plants, moss
> and natural hardscape are arranged to create depth and variation.

**Avoid**

> An exquisite, thoughtfully curated, beautifully balanced botanical
> masterpiece, designed to redefine the art of contemporary living.

Suggested lengths, not hard rules: - Hero supporting copy: 1--2
sentences. - Section introduction: 2--3 sentences. - Product
description: 2--4 sentences, depending on what needs explaining. -
Material or care explanation: as much space as clarity requires.

### Avoid repetitive patterns

A common generated-copy pattern is a sequence of identical sentence
constructions:

> Each vessel is selected with care. Each plant is chosen with purpose.
> Each detail is considered. Each creation is made to inspire.

A more natural progression:

> The vessel sets the boundaries of the landscape. Plant selection and
> hardscape are considered around its shape, scale and proportions.

## 7. Headlines and section titles

Headlines should be short, specific and connected to the section that
follows.

-   Use one clear thought.
-   Prefer concrete nouns and verbs.
-   Let the supporting paragraph provide nuance.
-   Do not use a poetic headline if the section is simply a practical
    explanation.
-   Avoid a string of abstract titles that look attractive but do not
    tell visitors what they will find.

Working examples, not approved final copy:

  Section purpose   Possible direction
  ----------------- ---------------------------------
  Hero              Forest. Reimagined.
  Philosophy        A closer view of nature.
  Inspiration       Drawn from the forest floor.
  Composition       A landscape in glass.
  Materials         The elements within.
  Plant selection   Chosen for the composition.
  Custom work       Made around your vision.
  Visit             See the compositions in person.

Use sentence case for normal headings and body copy. The brand line may
use uppercase as a deliberate visual treatment. Do not write entire
paragraphs in uppercase. Keep punctuation restrained.

## 8. Writing about terrariums

The website should help visitors understand what they are looking at,
not merely admire a photograph.

Where relevant, describe: - The glass vessel and its shape or size. -
The plants and moss used. - Natural hardscape, such as roots or stone. -
The substrate and its role. - How the elements are arranged. - The
overall landscape or visual character.

Do not list every material in every paragraph. Choose details that help
explain that particular piece.

### Explain the role of elements

A material list is not enough. When useful, explain why an element is
present:

-   The vessel defines the available space and influences the
    composition.
-   Hardscape introduces structure, height and direction.
-   Plants contribute form, foliage and growth.
-   Moss can create softer surfaces and ground-cover effects.
-   Substrate forms the growing medium and may include coco chips, peat
    moss, perlite and pumice.

Describe the actual build accurately. Do not imply that every
composition contains every listed material.

### Keep technical language accessible

Explain a technical term the first time it matters.

> Hardscape refers to the natural structural elements, such as roots and
> stone, that help give the landscape its form.

Do not assume every visitor knows terms such as *substrate*, *hardscape*
or *closed terrarium*.

### Avoid unsupported technical claims

Do not call a terrarium maintenance-free, guaranteed self-sustaining,
completely self-sufficient, zero-maintenance, suitable for every plant,
or guaranteed to thrive indefinitely.

Sylva Auris aims for low-maintenance compositions, not a promise that
the owner never needs to care for them. If care varies by plant, vessel
or setup, say so and direct visitors to the care information where
appropriate.

## 9. Writing about the experience

The central emotional idea is that a person can observe the character of
a forest without being in one. Treat this as an invitation to look
closely, not as a promise of wellness or an exaggerated escape.

Use specific observations: - Variation in leaf shapes. - Moss spreading
across a surface. - Roots creating structure. - Layers of foliage. - The
contrast between glass and natural textures. - How a miniature landscape
changes as plants grow.

Do not promise that a terrarium will reduce anxiety, improve mental
health, transform someone's life, create perfect tranquillity or
recreate an entire forest ecosystem. Unless there is reliable evidence
and a clear reason to make such a claim, describe the experience in
ordinary terms: a natural composition to observe, appreciate and care
for.

## 10. Writing about customisation

Customisation should sound collaborative and practical.

Relevant enquiry details include budget, intended use, occasion,
preferred size, vessel options, plant selection, hardscape and gravel,
and the customer's familiarity with terrariums.

**Working example**

> Custom compositions can be developed around the space, occasion and
> budget you have in mind. Share your preferred size and any ideas you
> have; we can discuss suitable plants, vessels and hardscape before
> shaping the final arrangement.

Do not suggest that every imaginable vessel, plant or material is always
available. Confirm availability before making a specific promise. Avoid
empty phrases such as "your imagination is our only limit".

## 11. Writing about gifting

The gifting proposition is that a terrarium is a lasting,
nature-inspired alternative to a short-lived bouquet. Keep the
comparison respectful and factual.

**Working example**

> A terrarium can be given for an occasion and continue to be part of
> the recipient's space long after the day itself.

You may mention that it is a gift that lasts, but do not imply it lasts
forever or requires no care.

Be careful with *sustainable*. A long-lasting gift is not automatically
environmentally sustainable. Do not make environmental claims about
materials, sourcing, production or impact unless verified.

## 12. Calls to action

The website introduces the brand, explains the creations, encourages
enquiries and helps people visit the physical location in Moodbidri. It
is not currently an online checkout or shipping storefront.

**For browsing** - Explore the collection - View the gallery - Discover
the compositions

**For custom enquiries** - Enquire about a custom piece - Discuss a
composition - Share your requirements

**For visiting** - Visit us in Moodbidri - See the creations in person -
Find our location

Avoid *Buy now*, *Shop now*, *Order online*, *Get yours today*,
*Limited-time offer* and *Don't miss out*. Do not create urgency unless
there is a genuine deadline or limited availability. Give each section a
clear next step; do not stack competing primary CTAs in the same view.

## 13. Page-by-page tone guidance

### Hero

Establish identity and give the visitor a reason to continue. Keep the
headline short, let imagery establish atmosphere, use one clear
supporting statement, and do not explain the entire business in the
hero.

### Brand philosophy

Explain what Sylva Auris is trying to create and why terrariums matter
to the brand. Use plain, thoughtful language. Explain the idea of
observing a forest in a smaller setting. Avoid generic claims about
passion, perfection or changing the world.

### Inspiration

Show the natural forms and environments that inform the compositions.
Mention tropical landscapes and forest-floor details where relevant.
Describe actual forms, textures and growth. Do not imply that every
piece recreates a specific habitat unless it does.

### Craft and composition

Make the work understandable. Name the vessel, plants, moss, substrate
and hardscape where relevant, and explain how they contribute. Be
transparent without turning the page into a technical manual.

### Collections and formats

Help visitors understand available options. Use the actual format and
size names used by the business. Explain differences in vessel size or
composition where known. Do not invent fixed dimensions, plant counts or
specifications.

### Custom creations

Explain what can be personalised and how to start. Be clear about what
can currently be customised. Explain the information needed for an
enquiry. Do not promise unlimited options.

### Kokedama

Introduce it as a distinct offering. Explain it in direct language; do
not force terrarium-specific descriptions onto it. Link to the dedicated
care information where appropriate.

### Visit and contact

Make the next step clear. State the physical location accurately.
Explain that visitors can see available options in person. Make the
enquiry route obvious. Do not imply online delivery or checkout is
available if it is not.

## 14. Claims and accuracy policy

The brand should sound confident because its descriptions are accurate.

### Safe to communicate when relevant

-   Sylva Auris creates terrariums and kokedama.
-   Compositions use living plants and natural elements.
-   The work considers the vessel, plants, substrate and hardscape
    together.
-   Customers can enquire about custom compositions.
-   Available creations can be viewed in person at the physical location
    in Moodbidri.
-   Terrariums are designed with low maintenance in mind, while still
    requiring appropriate care.

### Verify before publishing

-   Exact plant species in a particular product.
-   Exact dimensions and volume.
-   Specific material sourcing.
-   Environmental or sustainability claims.
-   Claims about lifespan or plant growth.
-   Availability of a vessel, plant or hardscape.
-   Any guarantee or care interval.
-   Any claim of being first, leading, best or unique in a market.

### Never invent

Awards, certifications, press coverage, customer testimonials,
production numbers, technical performance guarantees, environmental
credentials, availability, delivery or shipping services that have not
been confirmed.

## 15. Before-and-after examples

These examples illustrate the editorial direction. They are not final
approved product facts or website copy.

### Brand introduction

**Avoid**

> At Sylva Auris, we are passionate about bringing you exquisite,
> handcrafted botanical masterpieces that seamlessly blend nature and
> luxury.

**Prefer**

> Sylva Auris creates terrariums and kokedama inspired by the forms and
> textures of the natural world. Each composition brings living plants
> and natural elements together in a considered arrangement.

**Why:** It says what the brand makes and what informs the work, without
self-praise.

### Terrarium description

**Avoid**

> Discover a breathtaking miniature paradise, carefully curated to bring
> serenity and elegance into your life.

**Prefer**

> Moss, foliage and natural hardscape form a compact landscape within
> glass. The arrangement draws attention to the different textures,
> shapes and layers found on a forest floor.

**Why:** It gives the visitor something specific to imagine.

### Customisation

**Avoid**

> Let your imagination run wild. We create one-of-a-kind masterpieces
> that reflect your unique personality.

**Prefer**

> Custom compositions can be discussed around your preferred size,
> budget and intended setting. Plant selection, hardscape and vessel
> options can then be considered together.

**Why:** It describes the actual conversation and avoids overpromising.

### Gifting

**Avoid**

> Give the gift of everlasting nature and unforgettable memories.

**Prefer**

> A terrarium is a nature-inspired gift that can remain part of a room
> long after the occasion has passed. It offers something to observe and
> care for, rather than something intended to last only a few days.

**Why:** It communicates the lasting-gift idea without claiming
permanence.

### Materials

**Avoid**

> Only the finest materials are selected to create a flawless ecosystem.

**Prefer**

> The substrate provides the growing medium, while roots, stone and
> other hardscape elements help establish the landscape's structure.
> Their placement is considered alongside the vessel and plant
> selection.

**Why:** It explains the work rather than making an unverifiable quality
claim.

## 16. How to prevent AI-sounding copy

This is an editorial checklist, not a claim that AI can always be
detected.

### Remove common signs

-   Repeated three-part adjective lists: "beautiful, elegant and
    timeless".
-   Repeated sentence openings: "Each piece...", "Every detail...", "At
    Sylva Auris...".
-   Generic contrast formulas: "Not just a terrarium, but...".
-   Empty intensifiers: "truly", "perfectly", "incredibly",
    "exceptionally".
-   Abstract nouns stacked together: "beauty, elegance, artistry,
    harmony and sophistication".
-   Overuse of em dashes, colons and dramatic fragments.
-   Repeated claims that the work is carefully crafted, thoughtfully
    curated or made with passion.
-   A concluding sentence that restates the same idea in grander
    language.
-   Copy that describes a photograph without adding useful information.
-   Uniform paragraph lengths and identical section structures.

### Make the writing sound considered

-   Use details that come from understanding the actual creation.
-   Allow some sentences to be plain and practical.
-   Use a specific noun instead of an adjective whenever possible.
-   Read the copy aloud. If it sounds like an advertisement for any
    premium brand, revise it.
-   Remove sentences that add no meaning, atmosphere or useful
    direction.
-   Do not force a metaphor into every section.
-   Let some product descriptions be straightforward. Not every object
    needs a story.

### A simple editing exercise

Highlight a draft: - **Green:** facts and details specific to the
product or brand. - **Blue:** useful explanation for the visitor. -
**Yellow:** atmosphere or emotional language. - **Red:** generic praise,
repeated ideas or unsupported claims.

If most of a paragraph is red or yellow, it probably needs more concrete
information.

## 17. Editorial workflow

1.  **Identify the section's job.** What should the visitor understand
    or do after reading it?
2.  **Gather the facts.** Confirm the product, materials, process,
    location and available options.
3.  **Choose one central thought.** Do not make one paragraph carry
    several messages.
4.  **Write plainly first.** Explain the idea without trying to sound
    luxurious.
5.  **Add atmosphere selectively.** Use one or two real details that
    help the reader picture the composition.
6.  **Edit for rhythm.** Vary sentence length and remove repeated
    constructions.
7.  **Check claims.** Confirm every statement against what the business
    actually offers.
8.  **Read aloud.** Remove language that sounds unnatural when spoken.
9.  **Check the next step.** Make sure the CTA matches the section and
    current business model.

### Approval questions

-   Is this true of Sylva Auris?
-   Is it specific to the product or section?
-   Does it help someone understand or appreciate the composition?
-   Is the language natural when read aloud?
-   Have we used an adjective where a real detail would be stronger?
-   Have we repeated an idea already expressed elsewhere?
-   Does it promise something the business cannot guarantee?
-   Does the CTA reflect what a visitor can actually do today?

## 19. Final standard

The strongest Sylva Auris copy will not try to convince visitors that
the brand is refined. It will demonstrate refinement through the way it
notices, describes and explains the work.

**Write less about how extraordinary the product is. Write more about
what is actually there, why it is arranged that way, and what the
visitor can discover by looking closer.**

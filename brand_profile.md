## 1. Brand overview

Sylva Auris is a terrarium and kokedama brand based in Moodbidri,
Karnataka. It creates botanical compositions that bring living plants
and natural elements together in carefully arranged miniature
landscapes.

The brand is built around the idea of observing the character of a
forest in a smaller setting: noticing the forms of plants, the textures
of moss, the structure of roots and stone, and the way these elements
come together within a vessel.

Sylva Auris is not intended to be presented as an ordinary plant nursery
or a mass-produced plant seller. Its focus is on the composition: the
relationship between the vessel, plants, substrate, hardscape, scale and
overall arrangement.

The brand aims to make terrariums more familiar and appreciated, while
developing a recognisable premium identity for considered botanical
compositions.

### Brand in brief

-   **Name:** Sylva Auris
-   **Name meaning:** "The Golden Forest", derived from Latin words.
-   **Tagline:** "Forest. Reimagined."
-   **Category:** Terrariums and kokedama.
-   **Location:** Moodbidri, Karnataka, India.
-   **Core offering:** Ready-made terrariums and kokedama, along with
    custom compositions.
-   **Core idea:** A miniature natural landscape that can be observed
    and appreciated within an everyday space.
-   **Brand ambition:** Become a recognised premium terrarium brand in
    India and introduce new terrarium techniques and designs.
-   **Positioning direction:** Refined, composition-led, nature-focused
    and transparent about materials and construction.

## 2. Name and brand meaning

**Sylva Auris** is intended to mean **"The Golden Forest"**, combining
the ideas of forest and gold.

The name brings together two central parts of the brand:

-   **Forest:** the natural forms, textures, plant life and landscapes
    that inspire the compositions.
-   **Gold:** the sense of refinement, considered design and premium
    presentation associated with the brand.

The name should not be used as an excuse for excessive gold styling or
ornate language. The visual and verbal identity should remain
restrained. Gold is an accent and a symbol of refinement; the natural
compositions remain the focus.

### Tagline

**Forest. Reimagined.**

The tagline expresses the idea of taking inspiration from the natural
world and composing it in a different scale and setting. It should
remain a concise brand signature rather than being repeated in every
section of the website.

## 3. Purpose, vision and ambition

### Purpose

To introduce more people to terrariums and the possibilities of
miniature botanical landscapes, while creating compositions that bring a
considered natural element into the spaces people inhabit.

### Long-term ambition

-   Build Sylva Auris into a recognised premium terrarium brand in
    India.
-   Develop and introduce new terrarium techniques and design
    approaches.
-   Increase awareness of terrariums as a distinct botanical and design
    offering.
-   Build a brand associated with thoughtful composition, product
    understanding and refined presentation.

These are ambitions, not claims that the brand has already achieved
national recognition or market leadership.

### What the brand wants people to understand

A terrarium is not simply a plant placed inside a glass container. The
vessel, plant selection, substrate, hardscape and arrangement all
contribute to the finished landscape.

Customers should be able to understand what a composition contains and
how its elements contribute to the whole. Transparency about the product
is important.

### Desired customer impression

The central emotional idea is:

> A well-composed terrarium should make someone feel as though they are
> observing a forest without actually being in one.

The experience should encourage people to look closely and appreciate
the miniature landscape. Avoid turning this into unsupported claims
about health, therapy or guaranteed relaxation.

## 4. Brand positioning

### What Sylva Auris is

-   A terrarium and kokedama brand.
-   A composition-led botanical design business.
-   A brand that works with living plants and natural elements.
-   A place to explore ready-made creations and enquire about custom
    work.
-   A brand that values transparency about the contents and construction
    of its terrariums.

### What Sylva Auris is not intended to be

-   A conventional nursery catalogue.
-   A mass-production plant seller.
-   A generic home décor store.
-   An online plant-shipping business at present.
-   A brand that relies on exaggerated claims such as maintenance-free
    or guaranteed self-sustaining.

### Premium positioning

The intended premium quality should be communicated through the finished
composition and the care taken in arranging it, rather than through
repeated use of words such as "luxury", "exclusive" or "exquisite".

The desired result is a refined, elegant finished composition. This does
not mean making unsupported claims about expensive materials or implying
that every component is costly.

### Broad audience

The brand's intended audience is broad: terrariums can appeal to people
with different budgets, spaces and levels of familiarity with the hobby.

Potential customer motivations include:

-   Adding a natural composition to a home, desk or workplace.
-   Discovering terrariums as a new concept.
-   Finding a nature-inspired gift.
-   Exploring a hobby or learning more about miniature landscapes.
-   Commissioning a composition for a particular space, budget or
    occasion.

Do not define the audience so narrowly that the brand appears accessible
only to serious hobbyists or high-income buyers.

## 5. Products and offerings

Sylva Auris currently focuses on two main product categories.

### 5.1 Terrariums

Terrariums are the central offering. They bring living plants and
natural elements together within a vessel to form a miniature landscape.

The design is influenced by: - The shape and proportions of the
vessel. - Plant selection and compatibility. - Moss and foliage. -
Substrate composition. - Hardscape such as roots, wood and stone. - The
placement, scale and relationship of each element.

The objective is to create a coherent composition rather than simply fit
as many plants or decorative materials as possible into a container.

### 5.2 Kokedama

Kokedama is a separate offering within the brand. It should be
introduced and described on its own terms rather than treated as another
terrarium format.

A dedicated care page or care section is intended for kokedama. Keep
care instructions specific to the actual product and do not assume
terrarium care guidance applies.

### 5.3 Ready-made and custom work

The business offers ready-made creations and accepts enquiries for
custom orders.

Ready-made products allow visitors to see and choose from available
compositions. Custom enquiries allow the customer and Sylva Auris to
discuss a composition suited to a particular requirement.

Do not imply that every possible customisation is always available.
Confirm the availability of materials, vessels and plants before
promising a specific build.

## 7. Materials and composition

Transparency about the composition is a central part of the brand
experience.

### 7.1 Plants

Plants used or considered in the brand's compositions include:

-   Fittonia albivenis.
-   Raphidophora.
-   Hoya.
-   Ferns.
-   Peacock ferns.
-   Mosses.
-   Needle grass.
-   Other suitable tropical and shade-adapted plants.

This is a working list of plants discussed for the business, not a
guarantee that every plant is currently in stock or used in every
terrarium.

Use correct botanical names when confirmed. Avoid listing a plant on a
product page unless it is actually present in that product.

### 7.2 Moss

Moss contributes texture and can create softer, ground-cover-like
surfaces within a composition. It is one of the visual elements that
helps communicate the character of a forest floor.

Do not imply that all moss types have identical care requirements.

### 7.3 Substrate

Substrate materials discussed for Sylva Auris include:

-   Coco chips.
-   Peat moss.
-   Perlite.
-   Pumice.

The substrate forms the growing medium. The exact combination and
proportions may vary by build and should not be presented as a universal
recipe unless the business confirms a standard.

### 7.4 Hardscape

Hardscape provides structure and helps shape the miniature landscape.
Materials discussed include:

-   Red Moor roots.
-   Mangium wood.
-   Stone.
-   Gravel.
-   Other natural structural elements, depending on availability and
    suitability.

A previous material preference specifies mangium shelf wood rather than
walnut. Do not substitute walnut in descriptions or procurement
assumptions.

The exact hardscape depends on the design and the materials available
for the particular order.

### 7.5 Vessels and glass

The vessel is not merely packaging. Its shape, proportions and visual
character influence the composition.

The business has discussed offering or exploring different glass
vessels, sizes and shapes. Future ambitions include expanding options
around glass quality, vessel size and shape.

Do not describe future vessel options as currently available unless
confirmed.

### 7.6 Natural-material principle

The intended terrarium compositions use natural elements and living
plants, rather than artificial decorative components. Product
descriptions should reflect the actual contents.

Do not make broad environmental or sustainability claims merely because
the composition uses natural materials.

## 8. Design approach and making philosophy

The design process is vessel-led and composition-focused.

A central principle is to use the vessel's shape and design to determine
how the landscape should be arranged. Hardscape, gravel and plant
selection are considered in relation to the available space and the
desired composition.

### Design considerations

1.  **Vessel:** Establishes the boundaries, proportions and viewing
    angles.
2.  **Hardscape:** Introduces structure, height, direction and visual
    interest.
3.  **Substrate:** Provides the growing medium appropriate to the build.
4.  **Plants and moss:** Bring foliage, texture, colour and living
    growth.
5.  **Arrangement:** Brings the elements together into a coherent
    miniature landscape.

The goal is not to force every product into the same template. The
composition should make use of the vessel and the materials available.

### Customisation currently discussed

-   Hardscape.
-   Gravel.
-   Plants.

### Future customisation ambitions

-   More glass-quality options.
-   More vessel sizes.
-   More vessel shapes.

Treat future ambitions as planned possibilities, not guaranteed current
catalogue options.

## 9. Care philosophy

Sylva Auris aims to create **low-maintenance** compositions.

This wording matters. The brand should not promise that terrariums
are: - Maintenance-free. - Completely self-sustaining. - Guaranteed to
thrive indefinitely. - Suitable for every environment without care.

A terrarium is a living arrangement. Appropriate care may depend on the
plants, vessel, setup and surrounding conditions.

### Care communication

-   Give clear, practical instructions.
-   Keep advice specific to the product or setup.
-   Avoid universal watering schedules unless they are appropriate and
    verified.
-   Do not use "self-sustaining" as a blanket description.
-   Direct visitors to the relevant care information.
-   Keep kokedama care separate from terrarium care.

-   Space where the piece will be placed.

gravel. Vessel options may be discussed subject to availability.

### Enquiry experience

The website should make it easy to start an enquiry, but should not
imply an instant online product configuration or checkout process unless
such a system is implemented.

The enquiry flow should allow the customer to review and confirm their
details before an email is sent. Avoid a design that immediately opens
an email client without a clear review/confirmation step.

### Materials procurement and costing constraint

A previous operating constraint states that materials for a custom
catalogue or sample should not be purchased speculatively in large
quantities. Materials may be sourced after a customer order, and
availability should be confirmed before procurement.

This matters for any future customisation interface, product catalogue
or quotation tool: do not promise a material or option until
availability and cost are checked.

## 11. Gifting

Terrariums are being considered as gifts for a wide range of occasions.

The key proposition is that a terrarium is a nature-inspired gift that
can remain part of a recipient's space after the occasion, unlike a
bouquet that may last only a few days.

Potential gifting contexts discussed include: - Personal occasions. -
College events. - Company events. - Institutional gifting. - Hotel or
guest gifts. - Event giveaways or guest keepsakes.

The business has explored small jar terrariums for bulk gifting. A
retail price around ₹700 for a small jar format was discussed, but this
should be treated as a planning reference rather than a final universal
price. Bulk pricing remains unresolved and must depend on costs,
quantities and the specific design.

### Gifting language

It is acceptable to describe a terrarium as a lasting, nature-inspired
gift. Do not say that it lasts forever or requires no care.

Use the word "sustainable" carefully. Longevity alone does not prove
environmental sustainability. Avoid environmental claims unless the
relevant sourcing, materials and impact are verified.

## 13. Audience and use cases

The intended audience is broad. The brand should not suggest that
terrariums are only for experts or collectors.

### Audience motivations

**People furnishing a space** - Want a natural element for a desk,
shelf, table or room. - Appreciate a considered object with living
plants.

**People discovering terrariums** - May not know much about
terrariums. - Need simple explanations of what they are and how they
work.

**Plant and terrarium hobbyists** - May care about plant selection,
substrate, hardscape, vessel form and care. - May want a more tailored
composition.

**Gift buyers** - Want a distinctive, nature-inspired gift. - May be
buying for a personal occasion, institution or event.

**Organisations and event planners** - May need multiple small pieces
for events, guests, employees or institutional gifting. - Need clarity
on quantity, lead time, design consistency, price and availability.

Do not assume all visitors have the same knowledge or budget. Explain
specialist details without talking down to experienced hobbyists.

## 15. Visual identity and website design direction

This section records the established direction for the website. It is
included because visual presentation and brand language should work
together.

### 15.1 Colour palette

Current preferred palette:

-   **Pitch black:** `#000000`
-   **Muted gold:** `#B99868`
-   **Subtle ivory:** `#EAE4D8`

Natural greens should come from photography and the botanical subject
matter, not from green-tinted interface panels.

Avoid: - Charcoal panels as a substitute for pure black. - Green-tinted
UI surfaces. - Unnecessary decorative colours. - Heavy borders and
visual clutter.

Earlier palette explorations included `#0B0D0B`, `#161916`, `#8C7550`
and `#A8A89C`. The latest direction prioritises pure black, muted gold
and ivory.

### 15.2 Typography

Preferred direction: - Elegant serif typography for major headlines. -
Cormorant Garamond has been preferred for the redesign. - A clean
sans-serif for body text and interface details, with DM Sans as a
reference direction. - Earlier explorations included Cinzel and EB
Garamond for different brand/web projects; do not assume those are the
final Sylva Auris website fonts.

### 15.3 Overall design character

-   Dark luxury.
-   Classic, clean and contemporary.
-   Minimal distractions.
-   Strong product and botanical photography.
-   Clear hierarchy.
-   Refined motion rather than decorative animation.
-   No generic ecommerce-card appearance.

### 15.4 Website experience

The current redesign direction is an immersive, scroll-driven story that
gradually reveals the relationship between a forest landscape and a
terrarium.

Working narrative: 1. **Forest entrance:** introduce "Forest.
Reimagined." and establish the atmosphere. 2. **Botanical discovery:**
bring attention to moss, ferns, roots and foliage. 3.
**Transformation:** allow the forest-floor imagery to resolve into a
glass terrarium, with visual continuity between the natural scene and
the finished piece. 4. **Craft and composition:** explain the vessel,
substrate, plants and hardscape. 5. **Custom work:** introduce the
possibility of a composition developed around a customer's requirements.
6. **Brand close:** finish with a restrained brand signature.

This is a design direction, not a final locked page structure. The key
requirement is that the forest-to-terrarium transition should feel
continuous, not like unrelated images placed next to each other.

### 15.5 Motion and usability

Motion ideas discussed: - Layered parallax. - Moving light. - Restrained
dust or particle effects. - Macro camera movement. - Text reveals. -
Image masks. - Scroll-linked scaling and opacity. - One or two pinned
scenes, followed by normal scrolling for usability.

Avoid: - Excessive animation. - Sparkles and generic effects. - Abrupt
scene changes. - Overly dramatic light beams. - Distracting cursor
effects. - Motion that makes the site difficult to use.

Mobile layouts should simplify the experience and respect reduced-motion
preferences.

### 15.6 Existing website features to preserve

The existing site has included or planned: - Homepage. - Gallery. - Care
information. - Enquiry flow.

The redesign should preserve relevant functionality. The enquiry process
should let users verify and confirm their information before sending.

## 17. Photography and product presentation

The product should be shown clearly enough for customers to understand
the complete composition.

### Photography priorities

-   Show the complete terrarium, not only close-up details.
-   Include close-ups of meaningful elements such as moss, roots,
    plants, substrate layers and vessel details where appropriate.
-   Use consistent lighting and image treatment.
-   Keep the actual product recognisable.
-   Use the brand's own product photography for real product listings
    and gallery items.
-   Do not use illustrative or generated images as though they are
    photographs of an actual available product.

### Visual storytelling

The forest imagery used in the immersive opening should connect visually
to the real terrarium. Similar forms, textures and landscape geometry
can make the transition feel believable.

A generic rainforest photograph followed by an unrelated jar image will
weaken the story. The visual relationship should be deliberate.

## 20. Operations and constraints

### 20.1 Procurement

A previous operating preference was to avoid purchasing expensive
samples or large quantities of materials before there is a confirmed
customer requirement.

For custom or less common materials: - Confirm availability first. -
Check the cost before quoting. - Avoid assuming a material can be
sourced immediately. - Keep customer-facing options aligned with what
can actually be procured.

### 20.2 Fragility and delivery

Terrariums are fragile, and the risk of breakage or damage during
shipping has been a reason for not offering online delivery.

Do not add delivery promises or shipping rates to the website without a
tested process and a deliberate change in business policy.

### 20.3 Pricing

The business has discussed different price levels and product ranges,
but not all previously discussed figures are final.

Treat each price as one of: - Confirmed current retail price. -
Quotation for a specific custom order. - Indicative planning price. -
Unvalidated idea.

Never present an indicative or historical price as the current price
without confirmation.

### 20.4 Inventory and availability

Plants, vessels, gravel and hardscape may vary in availability. A
website catalogue should distinguish: - Available now. - Made to
order. - Custom enquiry. - Temporarily unavailable.

Only use these labels if the business has a reliable way to keep them
updated.

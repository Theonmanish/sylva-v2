## Read this first: status of the care guidance [INTERNAL]

The brand documents contain **no care instructions**. They say only that terrariums are designed with low maintenance in mind, that care depends on plants, vessel, setup and surroundings, that no universal watering schedule should be given unless verified, that kokedama care must be kept separate from terrarium care, and that the business must confirm care instructions for each terrarium type and for kokedama.

The copy below is therefore **general, widely accepted houseplant and terrarium guidance**, written as principles and things to observe. It is not drawn from the business's own methods and has not been checked against Sylva Auris pieces. It is a draft for the business to verify and correct, and, because it concerns living plants that customers will rely on, an experienced grower or horticulturist should review it.

**What this draft deliberately does not do**

- It gives **no watering intervals, quantities, light measurements, temperatures or humidity figures.**
- It makes **no claim** that any piece is maintenance-free, self-sustaining or guaranteed to last.
- It states **no plant-specific requirement** for any species, because no species is confirmed for any piece.
- It does not assume whether pieces are open or closed, or whether a vessel has a lid. Where this matters, the copy is written conditionally.
- It does not describe how kokedama is made or displayed beyond what is general.

**Before publishing, the business must:**

1. Confirm which vessel types it makes (open, closed or lidded, and any other form), and which statements apply to each.
2. Confirm or correct every statement in the verification table in section 8.
3. Provide product-specific care notes for each listed piece (see the template in section 6).
4. Confirm how kokedama is made and displayed, and the correct watering method.
5. Have the guidance reviewed by a suitably experienced person. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

## Section 02: Terrarium care

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** Terrariums
**Main heading:** Terrarium care

#### Light

Most terrarium plants suit bright, indirect light. Direct sun can heat the glass and scorch leaves. Too little light tends to produce thin, stretched growth and pale foliage. A quarter turn now and then helps growth stay even.

#### Watering

Terrariums generally need less water than potted plants, and an open vessel dries out faster than a closed one. Most glass vessels have no drainage holes, so excess water has nowhere to go and can collect at the base.

Check the substrate, the growing medium, before adding water. If it looks and feels moist, wait. If it is drying out, add a small amount of water at the base and let it absorb before adding more. Avoid leaving water pooled at the bottom.

#### Moisture and condensation

A light mist on the glass, especially in the morning, is normal in a closed vessel. Condensation that stays on the glass all day suggests there is too much moisture. If there is no condensation and the substrate is dry, the piece may need a little water.

#### Ventilation

If your vessel has a lid or cover, leaving it open for a period lets excess moisture escape. A vessel with an open top needs no lid management, but it dries out faster and may need watering more often. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: applicable vessel types]

#### Placement

Choose a spot that stays steady: bright, away from direct sun, and away from heaters, air-conditioning vents and drafts. Place the vessel on a level, stable surface. Glass is fragile, so lift the vessel from the base.

#### Looking after the planting

Remove dead or yellowing leaves as soon as you see them, by hand or with clean tools. Trim plants that press against the glass or crowd their neighbours. Wipe the outside of the glass with a soft, damp cloth, and keep sprays and cleaning products away from the plants.

#### Common problems

| What you notice | What it may mean | What to try first |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy condensation that stays all day | Too much moisture | Open the lid if there is one, let the piece dry a little, and water less |
| Fuzzy or white growth on soil, wood or leaves | Mould, from high moisture and little air movement | Remove the affected material, improve ventilation and water less |
| Yellowing or soft, translucent leaves | Often too much water | Remove the affected leaves and reduce watering |
| Brown, crisp leaf edges or dry-looking moss | Too little moisture, or too much sun or heat | Move away from direct sun, and add a little water if the substrate is dry |
| Thin, stretched growth leaning toward the light | Not enough light | Move to brighter, indirect light and trim the stretched growth |
| Plants pressing against the glass | Growth | Trim to keep the composition clear |
| Small flying insects | Often linked to a constantly damp surface | Let the surface dry between waterings, and get in touch if they persist |

If a problem continues, or you are unsure what you are seeing, get in touch and send a photograph. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

## Section 03: Kokedama care

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** Kokedama
**Main heading:** Kokedama care

**Introduction:**

Kokedama is cared for differently from a terrarium. What it needs depends on the plant and on how it is displayed.

#### Watering

Check the moss ball by its weight and feel. A ball that feels light and dry needs water. One that feels heavy and wet does not. When you water, do so slowly until the ball is evenly moist, then let the excess drain before returning it to its place. Do not leave it standing in water. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: the correct watering method for Sylva Auris kokedama]

#### Moisture

The moss should stay slightly moist. It should not dry out completely, and it should not be waterlogged. Dry, pale moss and a very light ball both suggest it needs water.

#### Light

Many kokedama plants suit bright, indirect light, and the right amount depends on the plant. Check the care note for your piece. Avoid long periods of direct sun. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: per plant]

#### Placement

Water drains from the ball, so place it on a dish or tray. Keep it away from heaters, air-conditioning vents and drafts. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: display method]

#### General maintenance

Remove dead or yellowing leaves as you see them. Turn the plant occasionally so growth stays even.

#### Common problems

| What you notice | What it may mean | What to try first |
|---|---|---|
| Yellowing leaves | Too much or too little water | Check the weight of the ball, and adjust |
| Dry, pale or shrinking moss | Too little moisture | Water slowly until the ball is evenly moist |
| Fuzzy growth on the moss | Mould, from too much moisture and little air movement | Let it dry a little, improve air movement and water less |

If a problem continues, get in touch and send a photograph. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

## Section 04: Common questions

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** Questions
**Main heading:** Common questions

**How often should I water my terrarium?**
There is no single schedule. It depends on the vessel, the plants, the substrate and the conditions where the piece is kept. Check the substrate and the condensation, and add a little water only when it is needed.

**Can I keep my terrarium in direct sunlight?**
It is best to avoid it. Direct sun can heat the glass and scorch the plants. Bright, indirect light suits most terrarium plants.

**Why is there condensation on the glass?**
A light mist, especially in the morning, is normal in a closed vessel. Condensation that stays all day suggests too much moisture. If the vessel has a lid, opening it for a while lets excess moisture escape. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

**How long will my terrarium last?**
That depends on the plants, the vessel and how the piece is cared for, so no lifespan can be promised. Regular attention gives it the best chance of doing well.

**What should I do if a plant dies?**
Remove it promptly, by hand or with clean tools, so that it does not decay in the vessel. If you are unsure what happened or what to do next, get in touch.

**Can I move my terrarium?**
Yes. Lift it from the base, and avoid sudden changes of position from shade to bright sun.

**Are the plants safe for children and pets?**
That depends on the plants. Some common houseplants can cause irritation if chewed or eaten. Keep pieces out of reach of small children and pets, and ask about the plants in your piece. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

**Structure notes**

- This is the most detailed page on the site. Detail comes from real materials, named elements and photography. It does not come from claims of skill, passion or perfection.
- **The page describes terrariums only.** Kokedama is a separate offering. Its technique, materials and care are not documented in the brand sources, so this page makes no statement about how kokedama is made.
- **Order of stages.** The page follows the blueprint order (vessel, foundation, hardscape, planting, composition). The brand profile lists the design considerations in a slightly different order (vessel, hardscape, substrate, plants and moss, arrangement). The introduction therefore states that the stages inform one another and that the order is for explanation, not a fixed sequence. No section claims a physical build order. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: whether the business wants a fixed order stated]
- **No repetition.** Homepage section 09 gives a two-sentence overview. Philosophy section 04 gives principles. This page develops the elements one at a time and uses different headings from both.
- **Atelier label.** The word names the page. It does not imply a studio that visitors can enter. No copy mentions studio visits, workshops or watching the work being made.
- **Held back:** "Handcrafted", "bespoke" and "artisanal" are not used. They require confirmation that they describe the process. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]
- **Photography is central.** The blueprint calls for close-ups of materials and process photography. If real process photography does not exist at launch, use close-ups of completed pieces and describe nothing as a process image.

## Section 03: The foundation

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** The foundation
**Main heading:** What the plants grow in

**Body:**

Below the plants is the substrate, the growing medium that holds them and supports their growth. The materials discussed for Sylva Auris compositions include coco chips, peat moss, perlite and pumice. The mix varies from build to build and is not a single recipe.

A substrate appropriate to the build matters as much as the planting above it.

**Material notes (publish only when the business confirms they describe its own mix):**

- Coco chips are a coarse, fibrous material made from coconut husk.
- Peat moss is a fine, moisture-holding organic material.
- Perlite is a light, white volcanic glass that opens up the mix.
- Pumice is a light volcanic rock that adds structure and air space.

**CTA:** None

### Internal notes

- **Purpose:** Explain the foundation, in plain terms, for readers who may not know the word *substrate*. It is defined at first use.
- **Accuracy:**
  - Substrate as growing medium, the four materials and the variation by build all come from the brand profile.
  - The material notes are general horticultural descriptions. The brand documents do not state the role of each material, so the notes are conditional. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]
- **Not stated:** Proportions, layer order, drainage layers, charcoal or any other element. None is documented. Do not describe layering unless the business confirms exactly how it builds the foundation. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]
- **Not stated:** Watering or moisture instructions. These belong in the Care page.
- **Visual:** Close-up photography of substrate materials, and of layers where visible through the glass. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: availability of real photographs]

## Section 04: The hardscape

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** The hardscape
**Main heading:** Structure from natural materials

**Body:**

Hardscape refers to the natural structural elements, such as roots, wood and stone, that give a landscape its form. It introduces height, direction and variation, and the planting is arranged in relation to it.

The materials used for hardscape include Red Moor roots, mangium wood, stone and gravel. Which of them appear in a piece depends on the design and on the materials available for that piece.

**Material notes (publish only the lines for materials the business confirms it uses):**

- **Red Moor roots:** Natural root wood that introduces height and direction. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: description of the root form used]
- **Mangium wood:** Natural wood used for structure within the composition. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: the form in which it is used]
- **Stone:** Adds weight and texture to the arrangement.
- **Gravel:** Finishes the ground of the composition. It is one of the elements that can be discussed for a custom piece.

## Section 05: The planting

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** The planting
**Main heading:** Plants and moss, chosen together

**Body:**

Plants contribute form, foliage and growth, and they have to work together. Shade-adapted plants suit the forest-floor character of the work, and plants that share a vessel need compatible conditions as well as complementary shapes.

Moss forms the softer surfaces: ground cover, texture and the transition between stone, root and substrate. Mosses differ in their needs, so care guidance is specific to the piece.

**Plants in compositions (publish only when confirmed as in current use):**
Plants used in compositions can include ferns, Fittonia and other tropical and shade-adapted plants, together with moss. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

## Section 06: Composition

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** Composition
**Main heading:** Where the decisions meet

**Body:**

Composition is where the earlier decisions come together. The placement, scale and relationship of each element are considered together: where the structure sits in the vessel, how the planting relates to it, and where surfaces of moss or gravel open up the view.

The same elements arranged in another vessel would give a different landscape. For that reason there is no single template. The vessel and the materials available shape each arrangement.

## Section 07: Customisation

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** Customisation
**Main heading:** Developed around your requirements

**Body:**

A custom composition can be discussed around the size, budget, occasion and setting you have in mind. It also helps to know how familiar you are with terrariums, so that care can be explained clearly.

Custom work begins with an enquiry. Plants, hardscape and gravel can then be considered together, and vessel options discussed, subject to availability. The availability and cost of materials are confirmed before anything is agreed.

**What can be discussed:**

| Area | Notes |
|---|---|
| Vessel | Options are discussed subject to availability |
| Size | Based on the size you prefer |
| Plants | Selection is discussed together with the rest of the composition |
| Hardscape | Roots, wood and stone, depending on availability |
| Gravel | Discussed together with the hardscape |
| Overall arrangement | Developed around the vessel and the materials |

Not every option is always available. The materials and plants to hand determine what can be offered.

**How a custom enquiry works:** [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: publish only when the business confirms the sequence]

1. **Share your requirements.** Tell us the size, budget, occasion and setting.
2. **Discuss the options.** Plants, hardscape, gravel and vessel are considered together.
3. **Confirm availability and cost.** Materials are checked before anything is agreed.

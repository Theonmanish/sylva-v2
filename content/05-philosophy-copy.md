## Section 02: The name

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** The name
**Main heading:** The Golden Forest

**Body:**

Sylva Auris is intended to mean "The Golden Forest". Forest is the starting point of every composition. Gold stands for care in the way it is arranged and presented.

**CTA:** None

### Internal notes

- **Purpose:** Explain the name briefly and without overreaching.
- **Source:** This text follows the approved wording in document 01 (section 14.2). It is unchanged.
- **Why "intended to mean".** The brand profile says the name is derived from Latin words. *Sylva* is sound as a Latin word for forest. *Auris* means "ear" in classical Latin, and gold is *aurum* or *aureus*. A statement that the name literally translates to "The Golden Forest" could be challenged. The copy therefore says only what the brand intends the name to mean and makes no etymological claim.
- **Recommended wording:** Keep the text above until the founder explains how the name was formed.
- **Alternative, only if the founder confirms the formation of the name:** Add one sentence after the first, for example, "Sylva draws on the Latin word for forest." and a second sentence about the second word, written to match what the founder confirms. Do not publish any statement about *Auris* until then. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]
- **Gold:** The text explains what gold represents in the name. It does not describe the visual use of gold on the site. Design explanations stay out of customer-facing copy.
- **Visual:** Typographic. The wordmark with generous space, and no imagery needed.

## Section 03: The concept

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** The concept
**Main heading:** The idea

**Body:**

In a forest, scale tends to take attention: the height of the canopy, the distance between trunks. A terrarium changes the view. At this size, the details become the subject: the pattern of veins in a leaf, moss spreading over a root, layers of foliage at different heights, the point where a plant meets stone.

The compositions do not attempt to recreate an entire forest ecosystem. They offer a small landscape to observe closely, and one that changes as its plants grow.

**CTA:** None

### Internal notes

- **Purpose:** State the central idea (observing the character of a forest in a smaller setting) as an invitation to look closely.
- **New copy.** The first paragraph is new editorial writing, not drawn from the brand profile. It is a perspective, not a factual claim about any piece. The founder should approve it as an accurate expression of the brand's thinking. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]
- **Specific observations used:** leaf veins, moss over root, layered foliage, plant against stone. These come from the tone guide's list of specific observations. They describe what a viewer may notice and do not state that any particular piece contains them.
- **Claims check:** No wellbeing, calm, escape or therapeutic language. No claim of an ecosystem. The second paragraph states plainly that the compositions are not ecosystems.
- **Growth statement:** "Changes as its plants grow" is general and makes no claim about rate or lifespan.
- **Visual:** Macro photography of moss, a leaf, a root or a layer of foliage. Real photography only.
- **Not repeated from the homepage:** The sentence "a person can observe the character of a forest in a much smaller setting" is used on homepage section 08. It does not appear here.

## Section 04: Design philosophy

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** Design philosophy
**Main heading:** Six principles

**Introduction:**

These principles guide how a composition is developed. They are principles rather than a template, because the vessel and the materials differ from piece to piece.

**1. The vessel**

The vessel is part of the design, not packaging. Its shape and proportions set the boundaries of the landscape and the angles from which it is seen. A tall, narrow vessel and a wide, shallow one call for different arrangements.

**2. Composition**

Restraint is part of composition. A vessel packed with plants and decoration has no clear structure. One arranged with space around its elements is easier to read and rewards a longer look.

**3. Balance**

Balance is the relationship between structure and softness. Roots, wood and stone carry weight, height and direction. Moss and foliage soften them. The proportion between the two, and their placement within the vessel, decides whether a composition feels settled or crowded.

**4. Plant selection**

Plants are chosen for their form and for how well they suit the composition and one another. Tropical and shade-adapted plants, ferns and moss suit the forest-floor character of the work, and compatibility within a shared vessel matters as much as appearance.

**5. Natural materials**

The compositions are made from living plants and natural elements: roots, wood, stone, gravel and substrate. Each has a role in the arrangement, and product pages name what a piece contains.

**6. Detail**

At this scale, small decisions are visible: how a root meets the substrate, where moss ends and stone begins, how much room a plant has to grow.

**CTA:** None

### Internal notes

- **Purpose:** Set out the principles that guide the work, as listed in the structure blueprint (composition, balance, plant selection, natural materials, attention to detail, vessel and contents).
- **Accuracy:**
  - Principles 1 and 2 restate confirmed design principles: vessel-led design, and a coherent composition rather than fitting as many elements as possible.
  - Principle 3 describes the roles of hardscape, moss and foliage in line with the brand profile and tone guide.
  - Principle 4 follows the plant-selection wording in document 04 (block B-PLANTS) and the brand profile's reference to tropical and shade-adapted plants. It names no species and does not say any piece contains them.
  - Principle 5 matches the brand profile. "Product pages name what a piece contains" matches the commitment in documents 03 and 04.
  - Principle 6 describes what is visible at this scale. It does not claim any process or practice the business has not confirmed.
- **The vessel example:** "Tall, narrow" and "wide, shallow" are generic shapes. They do not refer to named formats, because format labels are unconfirmed.
- **Terminology:** The word *hardscape* is not used on this page until section 05, where it is defined. Section 04 uses "roots, wood and stone".
- **Not stated:** Specific materials as available. Vessel, plant or material options. Process steps (these belong in document 06).
- **Do not add:** Claims that materials are sustainable, ethically sourced or rare.
- **Layout:** The six principles may be presented as a stepped sequence with generous spacing. No icons and no numbered badges are required.
- **Visual:** Close-ups of real pieces that illustrate principles: vessel proportions, the relationship between hardscape and planting, moss against stone. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: photography]

## Section 05: Brand perspective

### Website copy

**Eyebrow:** Perspective
**Main heading:** What Sylva Auris makes

**Body:**

Sylva Auris is a botanical design brand rather than a plant retailer. The work is the composition: how a vessel, plants, moss, natural hardscape such as roots and stone, and substrate come together into one landscape.

The contents of a piece are named, so that anyone looking at a terrarium can understand what it is made of and why. For many people a terrarium is a new idea, and the aim is to explain it plainly.

Kokedama, a separate offering, is treated on its own terms, with its own technique and its own care.

Terrariums are designed with low maintenance in mind. They are living arrangements, and they offer something to observe and care for.

**CTA:** None

### Internal notes

- **Purpose:** Explain how Sylva Auris differs from ordinary plant retail, as the structure blueprint requires, without criticising plant retailers.
- **Accuracy:**
  - "Botanical design brand rather than a plant retailer" follows the positioning in document 01.
  - "The contents of a piece are named" matches the brand's transparency principle and the commitment in documents 03 and 04.
  - "For many people a terrarium is a new idea" reflects the stated purpose of introducing more people to terrariums. It does not refer to any claim of leadership or market role.
  - The care sentence is the standard line from documents 02 to 04, with the addition "something to observe and care for", which follows the tone guide's wording for the gifting proposition.
- **Hardscape:** Defined here at first use ("natural hardscape such as roots and stone").
- **Not stated:** Ambition (a recognised premium terrarium brand in India, new techniques and designs). Ambitions are not claims and do not appear on public pages. No firsts, no market-position language.
- **Not stated:** Online sales, shipping or visit details. They belong on the Contact page.
- **Kokedama:** One sentence only. Kokedama's own description and care sit on the Collection and Care pages.
- **Visual:** None needed, or one restrained product image. The section is text-led.

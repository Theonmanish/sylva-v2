## 2. Positioning [INTERNAL]

### 2.1 Positioning statement

For people who want a considered natural element in their space, Sylva Auris is a botanical design brand that composes living plants, moss and natural materials into small landscapes within glass. Where a plant retailer sells a plant in a container, Sylva Auris designs the whole arrangement around the vessel and explains what each element contributes.

### 2.2 Core positioning line

Miniature botanical landscapes, composed around the vessel from living plants and natural materials.

### 2.3 What Sylva Auris is

- A composition-led botanical design brand.
- A maker of terrariums and kokedama, presented as two distinct offerings.
- Open about the contents and construction of what it makes.
- Available for ready-made pieces and custom enquiries.

### 2.4 What Sylva Auris is not

- A plant nursery or catalogue.
- A mass-production plant seller.
- A general home décor store.
- An online shipping business.
- A brand that promises maintenance-free or self-sustaining plants.

### 2.5 How premium is established

Premium positioning is shown, not stated. It comes from:

1. Photography of the real, finished compositions.
2. Precise description of what is inside each piece.
3. Restraint in language and layout.
4. Consistency across every page.

It does not come from repeating *luxury*, *exclusive* or *exquisite*. Prices are not yet confirmed, so no copy should imply a price tier.

## 3. Purpose, vision and ambition [INTERNAL]

**Purpose.** To introduce more people to terrariums and to the idea of a miniature botanical landscape, and to bring a considered natural element into the spaces people live and work in.

**Ambition.** To become a recognised premium terrarium brand in India and to develop new terrarium techniques and designs.

**Rule for ambition.** The ambition is a direction of travel, not a claim. Never write that Sylva Auris is "India's leading", "the first" or "pioneering" anything. The website may describe what the brand creates today. It should not describe what it hopes to become.

## 4. Central message [INTERNAL]

### 4.1 The idea

A person can observe the character of a forest in a much smaller setting.

This is an invitation to look closely at moss, roots, foliage and the structure of a composition. It is not a promise of calm, wellness or escape.

### 4.2 Core statement

> Sylva Auris composes living plants, moss and natural materials into small landscapes inside glass.

This is the plainest accurate sentence about the brand. Other copy can be more atmospheric, but it should never contradict it.

### 4.3 The tagline

**Forest. Reimagined.**

Usage rules:

- Appears in the homepage hero, the footer, the site metadata and the brand sign-off on printed material.
- Does not appear in body copy, product descriptions, care content or form messages.
- Stays as a brand line. It is never used as a section heading elsewhere on the site.
- Written in sentence case in running text. Uppercase is a visual treatment applied in design, not in the content file.

## 6. Message pillars [INTERNAL]

### Pillar 1: Composition, built around the vessel

**Message.** The vessel decides how the landscape is arranged. Hardscape, plants, moss and substrate are chosen to work within its shape and scale.

**Supporting facts.** Vessel-led design process. Hardscape considered in relation to available space. A terrarium is more than a plant in a glass container.

**Homepage sections.** 06 Brand Introduction, 08 Philosophy.

**Avoid.** "Perfect harmony", "balance of nature and art", claims that every piece is one of a kind.

### Pillar 2: Natural materials, named and explained

**Message.** Visitors can find out what a composition contains and what each element does.

**Supporting facts.** Living plants and natural elements. Substrate materials discussed: coco chips, peat moss, perlite, pumice. Hardscape discussed: Red Moor roots, mangium wood, stone, gravel. Moss provides texture and ground cover. Plants discussed include Fittonia albivenis, Raphidophora, Hoya, ferns, peacock ferns and needle grass.

**Homepage sections.** 09 Atelier. Detail on the Atelier and Collection pages.

**Rule.** Name only materials and plants that are actually present in the piece being described. The lists above are a working vocabulary, not a stock list. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION per product]

**Avoid.** "Only the finest materials", "flawless ecosystem", any sourcing or sustainability claim.

### Pillar 3: Two offerings, each on its own terms

**Message.** Terrariums are plants and natural elements composed inside glass. Kokedama presents a plant in a moss-covered form. They are different, and they are cared for differently.

**Supporting facts.** Kokedama has its own description and its own care guidance. Terrarium care advice is never applied to kokedama.

**Homepage sections.** 07 Collection.

**Avoid.** Describing kokedama as a type of terrarium, or reusing terrarium wording for it.

### Pillar 4: Custom work, discussed around the customer

**Message.** Custom compositions can be discussed around a person's space, occasion, budget and preferences.

**Supporting facts.** Enquiry topics: budget, intended use, occasion, preferred size, space, design preferences, familiarity with terrariums. Customisation currently discussed: plants, hardscape and gravel. Vessel options are discussed subject to availability.

**Homepage sections.** 09 Atelier, 12 Enquiries.

**Rule.** Do not promise a specific vessel, plant or material before availability and cost are checked. Materials are sourced after an order, not held in advance.

**Avoid.** "Your imagination is our only limit", "one-of-a-kind masterpiece", any implication of instant online configuration.

### Pillar 5: Honest care

**Message.** Terrariums are designed with low maintenance in mind. They are living arrangements and still need appropriate care.

**Supporting facts.** Care depends on plants, vessel, setup and surroundings. Guidance is specific to the product type.

**Homepage sections.** 11 Care (see note 15.3 on the section title).

**Avoid.** Maintenance-free, self-sustaining, zero-maintenance, "thrives indefinitely", any watering schedule not verified by the business.

## 7. Audience [INTERNAL]

The audience is broad on purpose. Do not write as though terrariums are only for hobbyists or only for high-income buyers.

| Segment | What they need to understand | Emphasis | Primary next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| People furnishing a space (home, desk, workplace) | What a piece looks like and where it can sit | Form, scale, finished composition | Explore the collection |
| People new to terrariums | What a terrarium is, in plain language | Explanation without jargon, honest care | Explore the collection, read care |
| Hobbyists and plant enthusiasts | What plants, substrate and hardscape are used | Material detail, vessel and plant selection | View the Atelier, discuss a custom piece |
| Gift buyers | A lasting, nature-inspired gift option | Gifting proposition, enquiry route | Enquire about gifting |
| Organisations and event planners | Quantity, consistency, lead time, price | Clarity, honesty about what is confirmed | Bulk enquiry |

**Writing rule.** Explain specialist terms (*substrate*, *hardscape*) the first time they matter, without talking down to experienced readers.

**Note on organisations.** Bulk gifting is an idea under consideration. Prices, minimum quantities and lead times are unconfirmed. Copy may invite a bulk enquiry. It may not quote terms. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

## 9. Terminology [INTERNAL]

Use these terms consistently in every file. Spelling follows British and Indian English: *enquiry*, *customise*, *colour*.

| Use | Meaning and use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Terrarium | Living plants and natural elements composed within a glass vessel | "Plant jar", "mini garden" (unless quoting a customer) |
| Kokedama | A separate offering. Describe it directly and do not call it a terrarium | "Moss ball terrarium" |
| Composition | The finished arrangement of vessel, hardscape, substrate, plants and moss | "Design piece", "masterpiece" |
| Vessel | The glass container, treated as part of the design | "Container", "pot" (for terrariums) |
| Hardscape | Natural structural elements such as roots, wood and stone. Define on first use | Using it without explanation |
| Substrate | The growing medium. Define on first use | Using it without explanation |
| Ready-made | A piece that has already been composed | "In stock", "Buy now" (availability unconfirmed) |
| Custom composition | A piece discussed and developed around a customer's requirements | "Bespoke" (use only if the process is confirmed as genuinely bespoke) |
| Enquire / enquiry | The route to ordering a piece or starting a conversation | Order, buy, purchase, add to cart |
| Collection | The set of ready-made terrariums and kokedama | Shop, store |
| Atelier | The page and navigation label for process and craft | Implying a public studio or workshop. Visits are not confirmed |
| Low-maintenance | The care positioning | Maintenance-free, self-sustaining |

**Format versus size.** The brand has used two different label sets: *Micro, Jar, Bowl, Cube* (formats) and *Micro, Medium, Large* (size categories). Keep them separate in every file. Do not use them interchangeably. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: final catalogue structure]

## 10. Voice summary [INTERNAL]

The full guide is `tone.md`. This is the working summary for every file.

- **Quietly assured, observant, informed, understated.**
- Write from the object outward: name the element, say what it contributes, then describe the impression.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs to adjectives.
- Vary sentence length. Let some sentences be plain.
- Atmosphere comes from real detail: moss, roots, leaf shapes, layers, glass.
- Do not use a metaphor in every section. Do not close with a sentence that restates the paragraph.
- Em dashes, colons and dramatic fragments are used sparingly.
- Proportions across the site: roughly a third confidence, a third atmosphere, a third information. Hero copy leans atmospheric. Product copy leans informational.

**Test for every sentence.** Could this appear on a hundred other premium brand sites? If so, rewrite it around a real detail.

## 13. Brand narrative

### 13.1 Brand narrative [WEBSITE COPY]

*Intended for the homepage Brand Introduction and, in expanded form, the Philosophy page. Draft for approval.*

Sylva Auris creates terrariums and kokedama in Moodbidri, Karnataka.

Every composition begins with the vessel. Its shape and proportions decide how the landscape is arranged: where a root rises, how moss spreads across the surface, which plants have room to grow. Natural hardscape gives the arrangement structure, and the substrate, plants and moss are chosen to work within the space.

The result is a small landscape that rewards a closer look. Leaf shapes vary, moss softens the ground, and wood and stone add height and direction. It is a way of observing the character of a forest in a much smaller setting.

Ready-made terrariums and kokedama can be explored in the collection. Custom compositions can be discussed around your space, occasion and budget. Terrariums are designed with low maintenance in mind, and like any living arrangement they need appropriate care.

### 13.2 Why this narrative is written this way [INTERNAL]

- It opens with a fact, not an adjective.
- It moves from the object (vessel) to its role (arrangement) to the impression (a closer look), as the tone guide requires.
- It contains no history, awards, numbers or superlatives.
- The final sentence states the care position plainly rather than leaving it out.
- The phrase "Every composition begins with the vessel" is accurate to the vessel-led design approach. Confirm with the founder that this holds for kokedama, which does not use a vessel. If not, change to "Each terrarium begins with the vessel." [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

## 15. Editorial notes and items requiring confirmation [INTERNAL]

### 15.1 The name: a Latin accuracy risk

The brand profile says the name is "derived from Latin words" meaning "The Golden Forest". *Sylva* (forest) is sound. In classical Latin, however, *auris* means "ear". Gold is *aurum* or *aureus*. Anyone with Latin knowledge could challenge a statement that "Sylva Auris" literally translates to "The Golden Forest".

**Recommendation.** Describe the name as *intended to mean* or *meant to evoke* "The Golden Forest" and do not claim a literal Latin translation. Keep the phrasing in 14.2 and carry it into the Philosophy page ("Meaning Behind the Name"). Ask the founder how the name was formed so the explanation can be accurate. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

### 15.2 Homepage section count

The Project Instructions define 12 homepage sections (five cinematic, seven on pure black). The earlier Website Structure Blueprint describes nine. Document 02 will follow the 12-section version in the Project Instructions. The blueprint's content areas (collection preview, composition, atelier, philosophy preview, closing enquiry) map onto sections 07 to 12.

### 15.3 Section 11 title: "Care and Longevity"

"Longevity" invites a promise about how long a terrarium lasts, which the business cannot guarantee. **Recommendation.** Use "Care" as the visible heading, or a heading about looking after the piece, and avoid any lifespan wording. The internal section label can stay as planned.

### 15.4 Format versus size labels

See section 9. The catalogue structure decides how documents 03 and 04 are written.

### 15.5 Enquiry flow conflict

The Brand Profile asks for a review-and-confirm step before an enquiry email is sent. The PRD describes a server-side submission with email delivery and confirmation after success. These are compatible (review, then confirm, then send server-side), but document 09 and document 11 must write the review step explicitly. Decide the exact sequence before writing form microcopy.

### 15.6 Location and visiting

Do not publish a street address, opening days, hours or display arrangement until confirmed. Historical references (Soans Farm display, a Google Business Profile address in Bannadka, Beluvai) may be out of date. The word "Atelier" must not imply a studio that visitors can enter. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

### 15.7 Premium positioning without prices

No prices are confirmed. Until they are, the website cannot imply a price level, and the Collection should be written to work with or without price display. [REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]

### 15.8 Information needed before later documents

1. Final catalogue: product names, formats, sizes, availability.
2. Exact plants and materials in each ready-made piece.
3. Confirmed prices, or a decision to omit them.
4. Whether kokedama is made to a vessel-free process (affects 13.2).
5. What customisation is confirmed today (plants, hardscape, gravel, vessels).
6. Care instructions verified by the business for terrariums and kokedama.
7. Current address, display arrangement and visiting hours.
8. Whether any delivery option exists.
9. Whether bulk gifting is offered, and on what terms.
10. How the name was formed (see 15.1).

### 15.9 What to avoid across every file

Generic luxury phrasing, repeated adjective triplets, repeated sentence openings ("Each...", "Every..."), "more than just" constructions (the long description in 14.1 uses *more than a plant in a glass container* once as a plain statement of fact; do not repeat the pattern elsewhere), unsupported claims, and any implication of online checkout.
